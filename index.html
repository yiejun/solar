Before the model looks

Clean up the picture first.

Drone frames arrive with glare, haze and noise. A short preprocessing pipeline makes contamination easier to see and features easier to extract.

    Raw frame

    What the camera actually sees: uneven light and a washed-out surface.

  2. 2

    Gaussian filtering

    Smooths sensor noise so real texture is not confused with grain.

  3. 3

    Histogram equalization

    Spreads the contrast so faint dust and droppings stand out.

  4. 4

Inside the model

Convolution and attention, sharing one small body.

Built on MobileViT. Convolutions go first: small kernels pick up local texture, like the grain of dust or the hard edge of a dropping.

Then the feature map is cut into patches, the tokens a transformer reads.

Attention stays local. Each patch looks at its neighbours inside a small window instead of the whole image, which keeps attention cheap.

Both views fold back into one compact representation, and the panel gets a verdict.

Small enough to fly. Sharp enough to see.

A lightweight, MobileViT-based model for spotting contamination on solar panels from drone imagery. It reduces downtime, keeps energy output up, and adapts to other drone inspection and monitoring work.
